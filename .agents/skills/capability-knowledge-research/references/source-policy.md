# Reliable Source Policy

Use the highest available tier that directly supports the claim:

1. Official product or language documentation.
2. Official repositories, RFCs, standards, and specifications.
3. Official documentation or repositories of established open-source projects.
4. Authoritative technical learning sites and official tutorial systems.
5. High-quality engineering blogs with identifiable authorship and concrete
   technical detail.
6. Technical community discussions.

## Evidence rules

- Prerequisites, definitions, and core learning structure require Tier 1-4
  support. Tier 5-6 cannot be their only evidence.
- Tier 5-6 may support engineering experience, pitfalls, trade-offs, and
  supplemental explanations. Preserve the publisher and do not generalize one
  anecdote into a universal rule.
- Open the source itself. Never cite a search result, generated summary, URL
  remembered from prior context, or a page that does not support the point.
- Prefer a small sufficient set of sources. Add a source only when it supports
  a distinct necessary claim or materially improves authority.
- Judge authority while researching; the persisted minimum source record keeps
  only the title and direct URL.
- A vendor page may be authoritative for its own product but not automatically
  neutral evidence for the whole field. Keep vendor-specific claims scoped.
- Use source notes for a short statement of what was used, not copied content.

## Research depth

Use the Capability canonical name to choose a minimum sufficient boundary.
Cover the smallest coherent learning structure that enables useful practice
and an acceptance judgment. Defer internals, adjacent platforms, and advanced
operations unless they are intrinsic to the Capability itself. Role-specific
priority and learning depth belong to Roadmap generation, not global Knowledge.
